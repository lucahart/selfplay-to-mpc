From Self-Play RL to Constrained Control Distilling DiffMPC Students with DAgger

Luca M. Hartmann Parinaz Naghizadeh
University of California San Diego
[arXiv] [Code]

Pipeline.

We add explicit, checkable constraints to a strong end-to-end driving policy without retraining it from scratch. A teacher is trained with self-play RL, its action head is swapped for a constrained differentiable MPC, and the new policy is distilled from the teacher with DAgger in the same simulator.

⟶
⟶

1. Train a teacher in self-play

An end-to-end MLP policy is trained with PPO in PufferDrive, where every car is controlled by the same policy. Training follows a staged curriculum on CARLA maps and ends with a fine-tune on a mix of CARLA and nuPlan maps. We use two teachers, a small teacher (1.1B steps) and a large teacher (7.9B steps).

2. Keep the backbone, swap the head

The student copies the teacher's encoders and backbone. Its linear action head is replaced by an MPC adapter, a linear layer that outputs the MPC's targets, cost weights and node weights, followed by a constrained DiffMPC solved with WarpMPC over a one-second horizon. The constraints are built from the scene, not learned: road boundary half-planes anchored on the route, and ellipses around the four nearest agents.

3. Distill with DAgger

The student is trained in the teacher's simulator with DAgger: it drives itself, and the teacher labels every state it visits. The loss is on the MPC's first control, and the gradient flows back through the MPC into the backbone. Distillation takes 2 to 5 GPU-hours, no more than 30% of the large teacher's training time.

End-to-end policies.

An end-to-end policy maps raw measurements straight to controls with one neural network: encoders compress the measurements into latent states, a backbone processes them, and a decoder outputs a trajectory or controls. There are three common ways to train it.

Imitation learning

The policy copies logged demonstrations. It is cheap, but small mistakes take the policy to states the data never covered, and errors compound.

Reinforcement learning

The policy learns from a reward by trial and error. In fast self-play simulators, where every car is driven by the same policy, it reaches very strong driving after billions of steps.

Distillation

A student learns to act like a trained teacher. With DAgger the student drives itself and the teacher labels every state it visits, so the student learns to recover from its own mistakes.

Training end-to-end MLPs with IL, RL, or Distillation gives strong performances, but none we can easily check: MLPs have no explicit notion of where the road ends or where the other cars are.

Differentiable MPC.

Model predictive control (MPC) plans a short trajectory at every step. It uses a model of the car to predict where each control sequence leads, picks the one with the lowest cost that respects the constraints, applies the first control, and replans at the next step. In its simplest form:

$$\min_{u_0,\dots,u_{T-1}} \; \sum_{k} \big\| x_k - \bar x_k \big\|^2_{Q_k} + \big\| u_k \big\|^2_{R_k} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k), \quad u_k \in \mathcal U, \quad x_k \in \mathcal X_k$$
$$\begin{aligned} \min_{u_0,\dots,u_{T-1}} \quad & \sum_{k} \big\| x_k - \bar x_k \big\|^2_{Q_k} + \big\| u_k \big\|^2_{R_k} \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k), \\ & u_k \in \mathcal U, \quad x_k \in \mathcal X_k \end{aligned}$$

The targets \(\bar x_k\) and weights \(Q_k, R_k\) say what the car should do, the dynamics \(f\) say what it can do, and the constraint sets \(\mathcal U, \mathcal X_k\) say what it must never do.

Differentiable MPC turns the solver into a layer of the network. The network predicts the targets and weights, the solver returns the plan, and the gradient of the first control \(u_0^\star\) with respect to the targets and weights is computed through the optimality conditions of the solve. The network and the MPC therefore train together with ordinary backpropagation, and the constraints stay explicit at every step.

Bounds.

The student's MPC has two kinds of constraints, both built from the scene rather than learned: road boundaries, which keep the car on the road along its route, and agent ellipses, which keep it clear of the four nearest cars.

Results.

Failure rate (collisions plus off-road per vehicle, lower is better) on 1000 CARLA, 3645 nuPlan and 1000 Waymo test scenes, for students of a small (4x512) and a large (4x1024) teacher. Bars are the mean over nine seeds with standard error, and the dashed line is the teacher. Waymo is never used for training and serves as an out-of-distribution test.

The constrained DiffMPC student is never significantly worse than the other students. It matches or beats its teacher on every test set, and on Waymo it fails 12 to 22% less often than the teacher, mostly by leaving the road less.

Training cost.

Adding the constraints is cheap compared to training the teacher. For the large policy, distilling the constrained DiffMPC student adds at most 30% to the teacher's training time on one RTX 5090. The students reach their best checkpoint within 10M to 60M labels, against 7.9B self-play steps for the teacher.

The small teacher trained in only 1 h, so for it the 2.8 to 4.7 h of distillation is longer than the teacher's own training: each MPC label costs about 8 to 10 times an MLP label.
